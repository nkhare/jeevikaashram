श्री रवीन्द्र शर्मा 'समाज व्यवस्था' 'उच्च शिक्षा' गुरुकुल

A place to continue learning after a gurukul, homeschool, or open-school education — without defaulting into the conventional system.

What it is

Most gurukul, homeschool, and open-school graduates reach a point where there's nowhere to continue except conventional modern institutions. This gurukul is built for exactly that gap — a higher-education path rooted in the study of Samaj Vyavastha (societal systems): village systems, caste and varna history, education systems, science and technology, rural social economy, and Indian-method entrepreneurship.

It's inspired by the late Guruji Shri Ravindra Sharma, who founded Kala Ashram in Adilabad, Telangana. Jeevika Ashram itself was founded on the same inspiration, with his blessing.

Why this gurukul

Guruji's own teaching held that making an idea land in society depends on three things:

  • What is being taught — the subject and its content.
  • Where it's taught — the environment and design of the place.
  • Who teaches it — whether the teacher's own life embodies what they teach.

This gurukul is designed around all three: daily immersive ashram life — gau-seva, farming, craft work, scripture study — taught by people living that life, not lectured from outside it.

What students walk away with

These are real outcomes from sessions already run in the one-month pilot, not aspirations:

  • A real understanding of how India was colonized over the years — and how that shapes daily life even now, not just history-book knowledge.
  • Genuine appreciation for what's already been inherited — skills, systems, ways of living — instead of assuming everything worth having comes from outside.
  • Patience and follow-through built through real responsibility: tending a cow shed, growing a vegetable bed, seeing a meal through start to finish.
  • Understanding a house from the ground up — foundation, plinth, materials — not just living in one.
  • Consistency and general well-being through daily practice, plus practical home remedies.
  • A real sense of small businesses that could be started from home to meet basic needs.
  • A craft learned well enough to teach someone else — skill, teamwork, confidence, and peer learning together.
  • The simple, uncommon experience of working alongside others toward something real.

Current phase: one-month pilot

Before committing to the full year-long program, we're running a one-month pilot to test the format with a wider group — 10 to 20 students, wider than the eventual year-round cohort will be.

This isn't theoretical — sessions are already running, taught through discussion and lived-in ashram work, not lecture. See what students are actually walking away with below.

Who's running it

Co-organized by Ashish Ji and Ragini Ji (Jeevika Ashram), Neependra Khare, Vivek Negi, Prateek, and Brijesh Ji.

The full year program, as proposed

  • Free tuition. Guru-dakshina is voluntary — given if and when a student is able to.
  • Run as a guru-family, not an institution — funded by donation and the ashram's own production, not fees.
  • Up to 5 students a year (7–8 in the first year), ages 15–24, extendable to 25 by mutual agreement.
  • A full daily rhythm from 4:30 AM to 10:30 PM — gau-seva, farming, craft work, two discussion sessions, scripture study, evening satsang.
